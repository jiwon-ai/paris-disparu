# ---------------------------------------------------------------- the stranger
[stranger_hello]
MAN WITH NOTEBOOK: Shh. I'm counting.

Three lorries. One cat. Two flat caps. One person in a raincoat.

Ah, you're the raincoat. I write down everything that passes in this street.

But one thing I can't explain. Every night, an apple appears on Simone's counter, at the café.

Nobody sees who leaves it. Simone would love to know. So would I.

My old notebooks are at home, by the phone. My wife will read them to you: 4127.

[stranger_knows]
MAN WITH NOTEBOOK: You know who it is. I can see it on your face. Noted. Go on, then.

[stranger_again]
MAN WITH NOTEBOOK: Ask Simone at the café. And call my notebooks: 4127. Somebody noted something.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- the porter
[porter_hello]
FERNAND: Mind your back, coming through! Out wandering at this hour?

Then make yourself useful. Three crates for the stalls, inside.

Each crate has its stall. Read the signs and you'll manage. Give me a hand?
? Yes | No

[porter_ask]
FERNAND: So, are you helping or just watching?
? Yes | No

[porter_yes]
FERNAND: Good. Take them from my cart, one at a time. Look at what's inside.

And lift with your legs, not your back. A back has to last a lifetime.

[porter_no]
FERNAND: Suit yourself. The crates won't carry themselves, though.

[porter_working]
FERNAND: Cart's here, stalls are inside. Go on, go on!

[porter_done]
FERNAND: Three crates, three stalls. Not bad for a beginner.

Go to the café on the street. Tell Simone that Fernand sent you.

And, er… don't tell her anything else. There's nothing else. Go on.

[porter_confront]
FERNAND: Kid. Why are you looking at me like that?

You tell him what you found. The apple, paid for at the stall. The yellow hat at three.

The coffee run that takes him ages, every single night.

Fernand goes red, right up to his hat.

FERNAND: …Yes. It's me. Every night I buy Germaine's best apple.

I polish it on my sleeve all the way to the café. Then I lose my nerve, leave it, and run.

I even bought a book about love letters. I couldn't write a single line.

Say… should I tell her myself? Now, before I lose my nerve again?
? Yes | No

[end_yes]
Fernand takes a deep breath and straightens his hat. FERNAND: Right. Now.

At the café, he puts the apple on the counter. Not in secret, this time.

SIMONE: …So it was you. Every night, for a year.

FERNAND: A year and a half. Since the cold winter.

Simone pours two coffees. She says nothing. She doesn't need to.

[end_no]
FERNAND: Not yet. Some things are easier to say with apples.

You keep his secret. Tomorrow at three, an apple will wait on the counter.

And Simone will smile, as if she didn't know. Maybe she already does.

[porter_after]
FERNAND: Still here? The café is on the street. Simone doesn't bite. Hardly.
