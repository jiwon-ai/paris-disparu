# Reading log: vanished Paris 1960s (raw notes)

Raw notes of pages actually read on 2026-11-23 (via a fetch tool; secondary sources unless stated). Quotes are in the source language. "Not stated" = the page does not say it. The curated database is docs/VANISHED_PARIS_DATABASE.md.

# Batch A (read)
- Bus platform: paris.fr bus history: "Leur dernier voyage a lieu le 22 janvier 1971", withdrawal of platform buses, last on line 21 (St-Lazare - Porte de Gentilly). 528M riders 1970 vs 852M 1961. Renault TN wiki: TN6 out 1969, TN4F 1970, TN4H 1971; platform use not stated.
- Vespasiennes (pariszigzag vespasienne-derniere): disappear progressively from 1960s, massively 1980s, replaced by JCDecaux sanisettes; last at 86 bd Arago (14e). 1960s count not stated.
- Luxembourg chairs (jardin.senat.fr the-seats): fee, concession contracts since 18th c.; 1955 standard model; 1974 Senate made chairs free, bought stock from last concessionaire. 1923 model. Chaisières NOT stated.
- Scopitone (en.wiki): 16mm film jukebox, patented 1958, Cameca Courbevoie; faded by end of 1960s; last film 1978. French café installation not stated.
# Batch B (read)
- Bougnat (en.wiki; pariszigzag bougnats): Auvergnats/Aubrac, firewood+coal delivery + wine; "Vins et charbons", cafés-charbons; golden age 1st half 20th c.; café-charbons have disappeared today; one bougnat still rue Émile-Lepeu. 1960s decline date not stated.
- New franc (retrowow): 1 Jan 1960, 1 NF=100 old; people still talked in old francs, "mille francs"; shopkeepers & waiters billed in NF but talked old; Giscard 1963 admitted still thinking in old francs; 1963 dropped "new" label. numiscorner: 1 Jan 1960; 1 Jan 1963 'nouveau' dropped; avg salary 61,000 -> 610.
- Phone letters (britishtelephones tel_hist_paris): 3-letter exchange + 4 digits e.g. CENtral 1234; Paris changeover 1 Oct 1963 new subscribers all-figure; letters kept on dials into 1980s; speaking clock ODÉon 8400; 8 digits 25 Oct 1985. Circular 21 Sep 1963 (histelfrance PDF): pre-Oct-1963 subscribers keep number provisionally; directories all-digits from 1965; ODEon=033, BALzac=225, OPEra=073, SEGur=734.
  => In 1967 both forms coexist: old subscribers still "ODEon 12-34"-style; new ones 7 digits. Directory digits from 1965.
- Jeton (numista 16650): PTT token 1937, copper-nickel 4.15g 18mm, deep square groove "P.T.T", engraver Bazor; demonetized (date not stated).
# Batch C (read)
- Villette abattoirs (sortiraparis 331385): built 1865-67, largest butchery complex; up to 4M animals/yr; closed 1974. paris.fr lieux disparus 2: inaugurated 21 Oct 1867; activities suspended from 1974; 1922 scandal. Les Halles: 1969 transferred to Rungis; 1979 Forum.
- Voie Pompidou (en.wiki): Pompidou opened 13-km expressway 1967; right bank car ban 2017 (paris.fr says 2018 start). paris.fr swimming: "the right bank expressway was completed in 1967".
- Colour TV (ozap): 1 Oct 1967 14h15 SECAM. Speakerines introduced 1935, until early 1990s. ORTF wiki: colour Oct 1967 on 625-line 2nd channel; ads introduced 1968; 3rd channel Dec 1972; licence fee.
- Deligny (sortiraparis 332277): quai Anatole-France, floating, from 1801; 1960s legendary swimsuit fashion shows; sank 8 Jul 1993. paris.fr swimming: Seine bathing banned 1923 (fine); ~20 floating pools end 19th c.

- Ouvreuses (simonnetavocat 2026 legal blog): name from keys to open loges (19th); tips were norm; Vilar TNP from 1951 fixed salary; persists in some private theatres; Th. Champs-Élysées ended tipping Sept 2023. Cinema tips not covered. paris.fr cinemas: Arlequin renamed 1962 by Tati.
- Concierge (TIME 17 Jan 1964, time.com/archive/6813310): 95% women; 80,000 -> 64,000 since WWII; cordon pulled at night, bill 1957 abolished, most doors now electric release; hours 6am-10pm 6 days; garbage, lock doors, pipes; rent-free loge.

# Les Halles layout, batch 1 (read)
- en.wiki Les Halles: fruit/flower/veg left 1969, butchers stayed; last operated 12 Jan 1973; Halle aux Blés 1763-69 "at the west end of Les Halles", Bourse de Commerce 1889; Saint-Eustache 16th c.; claims one pavilion to Nogent and one to Yokohama (Yokohama contradicted by others).
- Larousse: 1854 plan 14 pavilions, 10 done by 1870, later 12; ~40,000 m2; each pavilion central nave with cast-iron columns, double lantern roof; ~8,000 t goods/day; transfer decreed 1962, done March 1969; destroyed from 1971; one pavilion re-erected 1977 Nogent.
- pariszigzag Halles de Baltard: 12 pavilions, glass + cast iron; "deux blocs homogènes répartis autour d'une rue centrale"; bounded north by Saint-Eustache, west by old Halle au blé (Bourse de Commerce); pavilion 8 = eggs & poultry, saved to Nogent; 27 Feb-1 Mar 1969: 20,000 employees left; Rungis 13 km; destroyed 1971-73.
- napoleon.org Baltard Pavilion: east section 1854-58, west 1860-74; pav. 8 egg & poultry; demolition began 2 Aug 1970; pav 8 dismantled 1972, rebuilt Nogent 1976, opened 1977.
- paris.fr Ce que Paris doit à Baltard: pavilions housed butchers, fishmongers, cheesemongers, market gardeners...; glass, cast iron, brick, iron; 1852-1870; first pavilion poultry 1853; Saint-Eustache restored, reinaugurated 1860.
- paris.fr Gautrand: photographed demolition Aug-Oct 1971; photos at Musée Carnavalet.
# Batch F (read)
- Métro 1st class (pariszigzag metro-rer-premiere-classe): since 1900; 1st-class cars recognisable by yellow markings, generally at ends of trains; supplement modest; ended on métro 1 Aug 1991 (Slate: 30 Jul 1991 17h); RER A 1999. Slate: 1900 2nd 15c, 1st 25c, 1st ticket red or turquoise; leather banquettes vs wooden slats; abolished 1946, restored 1948; 1982 2nd allowed in 1st at peak.
- Bercy (en.wiki Wine warehouses): until 1960s blended wine enriched merchants, then decline; 1964 leases not renewed, modernisation prohibited; barrels by boat, later wagons-foudres; 9.48 km of track; 1979 arena; last cellars destroyed 1993.
- Vaugirard horse market (pariszigzag): moved to 15e 1907 next to Vaugirard abattoirs (since 1898); market closed 1976; halls destroyed early 1980s; horse-head gate rue Brancion; book market since 1987.
- VéloSoleX (en.wiki): mass-produced 1946-1988; 7-8M sold; popular with schoolchildren, students, plant workers; S3800 1966 most sold; black frame; "la bicyclette qui roule toute seule".
- Bal musette (INA fresques Dasavi00705): born early 20th c., mainly rue de Lappe (Bastille), Balajo; accordion + percussion + double bass; cramped, couples danced tightly; decline not stated.
- Bains-douches (INED 2017): 16 free facilities, 500 showers today; 67% of users lack shower. 1960s figures not stated.
- Kiosques (paris.fr): first 1857 Grands Boulevards, lit; Hachette bought AAP 1948. 1960s / criers not stated.

# Les Halles layout, batch 2 (read)
- fr.wiki Halles de Paris (first half verbatim): 12 pavilions planned; "deux groupes séparés par une rue centrale à ciel ouvert située au niveau du chevet de l'église Saint-Eustache"; each group's six pavilions linked by "rues couvertes"; "dix pavillons couvre une surface de 34 817 m2"; opened 1857, 1858, 1860, 1874; "le numéro 3 pour la viande, le numéro 9 pour le poisson"; fruit & veg sold "sur le Carreau, dans les allées couvertes et sur les rues alentour"; streets opened: rue du Louvre, Turbigo, des Halles, du Pont-Neuf, Berger, Pierre-Lescot extended to square des Innocents; 1971: "les six premiers pavillons situés à l'est de la rue Baltard" demolished; 1973 meat pavilions demolished; no. 8 (eggs & poultry) to Nogent; Yokohama got only the upper part of one structure.
- fr.wikisource "Les Halles centrales de Paris" (contemporary article c.1858-59, PUBLIC DOMAIN): numbered 1-12; west body 1-6 (under construction), east body 7-12 (finished 1858), separated by a 32 m boulevard; covered streets ~11,000 m2; original allocation: 1 pork/charcuterie/tripe; 2 poultry & game retail; 3 butcher's meat (96 shops); 4 poultry & game wholesale + auction; 5-6 fruit, greens, cut flowers, vegetables; 7 flowers & fruit retail; 8 greens; 9 fish (sea, freshwater, salted) auction; 10 butter/eggs/cheese auction; 11 oysters; 12 butter/eggs/cheese retail. Boundaries: rues de la Tonnellerie, Cossonnerie, Rambuteau, Petite-Friperie; Halle au blé "la tête de l'ensemble"; 600 gas jets east body. Pavilions 2,000-2,900 m2 each, 40,000 m2 enclosed.

# Streets, tickets, batch G (read)
- en.wiki Rue du Pont-Neuf: runs from Pont Neuf (south) north to Les Halles; 1854 decree planned street Pont Neuf - Halles; named 1867; part between rue Berger and rue Rambuteau "renamed the Rue Baltard in 1877", removed for the Forum; continues north as rue Montorgueil.
  => rue Baltard = the central N-S street between the two pavilion groups, from rue Berger (S) to rue Rambuteau (N).
- en.wiki Rue Rambuteau: from rue des Archives to rue Coquillière; created 1838, named 1839; 13 m wide.
- pariszigzag ticket history: 1958 tariff: havane 2nd class, green 1st; carnet 30 F 2nd / 45 F 1st (1958, old francs); 1967-70 end of first cycle of "alphabet tarifaire"; 1968 tickets valid on buses. paris.fr: 1951 2nd 20 F, 1st 30 F; 1973 magnetic stripe, tickets lemon yellow.
- pariszigzag old tickets found: punched by poinçonneurs until early 1970s; one punch = single, double punch = return; ads printed on ticket backs.
- Gauloises (en.wiki): launched 1910 by SEITA; traditional short, wide, unfiltered, dark tobacco; "peculiarly French shade of blue" pack; Disque Bleu March 1954. SEITA (en.wiki): former state tobacco monopoly; Gauloises & Gitanes 1910; private by 1999.
- PTT leaflet April 1963 (histelfrance): explains why new subscribers wait (free pair + exchange equipment needed). TIME 7 Jun 1976 "Rewiring France": waits up to seven years; "from 1935 to 1968 almost no new equipment was installed"; 1962 minister called phone a "gimmick"; 291,000 new orders 1968.
- Observatoire de Paris talking clock: born 1933 (Esclangon), world first; ended 1 Jul 2022; 1960s number not stated here (britishtelephones: ODÉon 8400 from inception).
- roger-viollet taximeter: photo captions only (1966 model in taxi; 1954 Peugeot 203 taxi). Weak.
