# Episode 2: historical research (27-28 February 1969)

Rule: each fact used in the game is listed here with its source. Anything
not sourced is marked **inference** or **fiction**. The characters (Fernand,
Mme Roux, Lulu, the old poet...) and their story are fiction.

## The last night of the market

| Fact used in the game | Source |
|---|---|
| The last day of trading at Les Halles was Friday 28 February 1969, in the early hours. People threw flowers. A lit bed of azaleas, roses and tulips stood outside Saint-Eustache. A band played and people danced round pyramids of crates. | AFP via [Malay Mail, 27 Feb 2019](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2019/02/27/50-years-since-iconic-paris-market-uprooted/1727367) |
| The move happened overnight on 2-3 March, with nearly 30,000 people and about 1,500 lorries. Rungis opened on Monday 3 March 1969. (Used in the epilogue: "1,500 of them, they say".) | Malay Mail (above); [paris.fr: Rungis et Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/rungis-et-paris-une-histoire-commune-6744) |
| Lorries lining up in rue Baltard on 3 March. | **inference**: the place of the convoy is not given in the sources |
| Saint-Eustache was built 1532-1637, so it had stood by the market for "more than three hundred years". | **inference** from the commonly given build dates; not checked against a primary source |

## Cité

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| The flower market on the Île de la Cité (place Louis-Lépine) dates from the early 19th century. Its iron pavilions are drawn closed at night. | [paris.fr: le marché aux fleurs de la Cité](https://www.paris.fr/pages/de-napoleon-a-elisabeth-ii-l-eclosion-du-marche-aux-fleurs-de-la-cite-16565) |
| The square in front of Notre-Dame was dug up from 1965 to 1972, before a planned underground car park. This gives the dig fence and the blue shard (the shard itself is fiction). | [Crypte archéologique: history](https://www.crypte.paris.fr/en/crypt/history-archaeological-crypt-and-excavations); [culture.gouv.fr](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/paris/en/archaeological-crypt-notre-dame) |
| Notre-Dame's spire stood in 1969 (it fell in 2019). The kings on the facade had heads again (19th-century restoration). | general knowledge, **not separately sourced** |
| Place Dauphine is triangular (begun 1607). Some brick-and-stone houses survive. | [Wikipedia: Place Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_Dauphine) |
| Cité station is on line 4, between Châtelet and Saint-Michel, and has a Guimard entrance. | [Wikipedia: Cité station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cit%C3%A9_station); [Wikipedia: Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_M%C3%A9tro_Line_4) |

## Métro

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Stop order on line 4: Étienne Marcel, Les Halles, Châtelet, Cité, Saint-Michel, Odéon, Saint-Germain-des-Prés. | [Wikipedia: Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_M%C3%A9tro_Line_4) |
| The line-4 Les Halles station of 1908 was replaced on 3 October 1977. Its **name in 1969 ("Halles" or "Les Halles") is not verified**. The game shows "LES HALLES". | [Wikipedia: Les Halles station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Halles_station) |
| Tickets were punched by hand (until the end of 1973). | Episode 1 research (docs/HISTORICAL_RESEARCH.md); [paris.fr: le ticket de métro](https://www.paris.fr/pages/le-ticket-de-metro-rejoint-le-poinconneur-des-lilas-24553) |

## Street life (Jiwon's request, from Episode 1 research)

Sources are in Episode 1's docs/HISTORICAL_RESEARCH.md, "Street life 2". They
still apply in February 1969.

| In the game | Status |
|---|---|

| Cats | Included as reconstruction (no 1960s source names cats at Les Halles; Jiwon accepted): asleep in rue de la Huchette, sitting in the Saint-Germain bookshop window. |

## Removed because they could not be verified

- That the dragons of the fountain spout water.
- "The oldest bell tower in Paris" (Saint-Germain-des-Prés).
- Chestnut trees in place Dauphine.
- The speaking clock's exact words ("At the third stroke"). The game uses only "...it will be exactly...".
