# Vanished Paris, 1960-1970: experience database

Purpose: things a player can **do, use, hear or see** in PARIS DISPARU that
existed in Paris around 1967 and have since disappeared or changed beyond
recognition. Each entry says what is sourced and what is not, so the game
never presents a guess as history.

Research date: 2026-11-23. Every URL below was actually fetched and read
(through a fetch tool; raw notes and short quotes in
[research/vanished-paris-reading-log.md](research/vanished-paris-reading-log.md)).
Earlier sourced facts are in [HISTORICAL_RESEARCH.md](HISTORICAL_RESEARCH.md).

**Limits, stated plainly.**
- Almost all sources are **secondary** (Paris.fr, Wikipedia, Paris ZigZag,
  Sortiraparis, press). Two are close to primary: a PTT circular of 21 Sep 1963
  (histelfrance.fr scan) and a c.1858 article on Wikisource. The **Gallica** copy
  of Baltard and Callet's book failed to load. Archives de Paris, INA (beyond
  one clip description), Carnavalet and RATP archives were **not consulted**.
- Confidence levels:
  - **A** = date/fact stated by two or more independent sources read, or one official/primary source.
  - **B** = stated by one secondary source read.
  - **C** = existence in 1967 is an inference from dates read (e.g. "ended 1974" ⇒ "existed in 1967"); the 1967 detail itself is not described.
  - **Not found** = looked for, no source read says it. Never state it as fact in game text.
- Copyright: facts are not copyrightable. Photos (Doisneau, Roger-Viollet, Gautrand), films, songs, posters and modern texts are. All game art must be original; no image is traced from a photo. Brand names (Gauloises, VéloSoleX, RATP, G7, Scopitone) are trademarks: generic names in game text unless legal review approves.

Ranking: entries are ordered by **gameplay value** (can it become a verb, a
puzzle, a mini-game or a quest step that fits a Game Boy Color screen and our
1967 Les Halles setting?), then by confidence.

## Part 2. Ranked experience database

Format per entry: **Period · Where · Conf.** — description; what changed;
sources; game idea; rights note.

### Tier 1: strongest gameplay value

**1. Métro ticket puncher (poinçonneur)** · until 1973 · every station · A
- Puncher in a booth or at the gate punched each ticket. **One punch = single ticket, two punches = return ticket.** Ads were printed on ticket backs. Gone with magnetic tickets (generalised Oct 1973; tickets became lemon yellow).
- Sources: HISTORICAL_RESEARCH (Paris ZigZag, Paris.fr, Sortiraparis); [Paris ZigZag, old tickets](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/des-tickets-vieux-de-70-ans-retrouves-dans-le-metro/); [Paris ZigZag, metiers](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/les-petits-metiers-insolites-de-nos-ancetres/); [Paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/le-ticket-de-metro-rejoint-le-poinconneur-des-lilas-24553)
- Game: already in (punch mini-game). Add: player must ask for "a return" → puncher punches twice; a clue hidden in the ad on the back of a found ticket.
- Rights: Gainsbourg song allusion only (see HISTORICAL_RESEARCH).

**2. Pneumatique ("petit bleu")** · 1866-1984 · post offices · A
- Message on a pre-franked blue form, shot through tubes to the office nearest the recipient, delivered by courier. ~4 million messages in 1960.
- Source: [Wikipedia, Paris pneumatic post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_pneumatic_post) (see HISTORICAL_RESEARCH)
- Game: already the Episode 1 hook. Add: send one yourself (write 3 words, it arrives "within the hour" — delivery time **not sourced**, keep vague).

**3. Two kinds of phone numbers at once (ODÉon 12-34 vs 7 digits)** · 1963-1965+ · whole Paris zone · A (primary)
- Before 1 Oct 1963: 3 letters of an exchange name + 4 digits (e.g. CENtral 1234). From 1 Oct 1963 new subscribers got all-figure 7-digit numbers; **existing subscribers kept their old number "provisionally"**; directories all-digits from 1965; letters stayed on dials into the 1980s. Conversion table: **ODÉon = 033, BALzac = 225, OPÉra = 073, SÉGur = 734, ARChives = 272**. Paris speaking clock was ODÉon 8400.
- Sources: [PTT circular 21 Sep 1963](https://www.histelfrance.fr/storage/2025/07/1963.09.21CirculaireIndicatifsChiffresZoneParis.pdf); [britishtelephones.com](https://britishtelephones.com/andyemmerson/tel_hist_paris.html)
- Game: **dial puzzle**. An old card says "BALzac 4127"; the player must find the dial letters B-A-L = 2-2-5 (period-correct). Replaces the current 4-digit simplification with a real mechanic. Fernand's old card vs Simone's new-style number = a generational clue.
- Rights: none (facts).

**4. Old francs vs new francs** · NF from 1 Jan 1960 · everywhere · A
- 1 NF = 100 old francs. People still talked in old francs; shopkeepers and waiters wrote bills in NF but talked old francs; "mille francs" meant 10 NF. In 1963 Giscard d'Estaing said he still thought in old francs. "Nouveau" dropped from 1 Jan 1963.
- Sources: [retrowow](https://www.retrowow.co.uk/retro_britain/old_money/old_and_new_franc.html); [Numiscorner](https://www.numiscorner.com/blogs/news/the-new-franc-the-story-of-a-reform)
- Game: **price-conversion puzzle**. Germaine says "two hundred francs"; the player must pay 2 F. Getting it wrong = comic dialogue. Strong, cheap to implement (text only).

**5. The night market itself** · until 1969 · Les Halles · B
- See Part 1. Memoir (1965): wholesale from 9 p.m., retail from 8 a.m. Thesis table (1960, via summary): fruit/veg from 3:30-4:00, fish 6:30, poultry and dairy 7:30. Meat cutters 1-11 a.m. Streets jammed with trucks; vegetables on the ground.
- Game: **time-of-night system**: some NPCs and stalls only exist in certain hours (e.g. fish pavilion opens at 4). Supports the existing 3:10 a.m. setting.

**6. Forts des Halles** · until March 1969 · Les Halles · A
- Licensed porters, leather coltin hat with lead cap, metal badge with Paris arms. "En mars 1969, le transfert ... vers Rungis sonne le glas de leur corporation." One memoir: a fort could carry 400 lb for 65 yards.
- Sources: HISTORICAL_RESEARCH (Paris ZigZag); [Paris.fr, métiers oubliés](https://www.paris.fr/pages/ces-metiers-oublies-des-rues-de-paris-7409); Bonjour Paris
- Game: Fernand already. Add: **carry mini-game** (balance a load through a covered street; timing). Load figure from one memoir only: don't print it.

**7. Onion soup at night / night brasseries** · 1947-1969 (as market ritual) · Les Halles · A
- Gratinée eaten midnight-8 a.m. by workers and night owls; Au Pied de Cochon open non-stop since 1947; soup "from midnight, after the shows let out".
- Sources: HISTORICAL_RESEARCH (Paris ZigZag); Sortiraparis Pied de Cochon; Bonjour Paris
- Game: **two crowds at one counter** (porters at 4 a.m., theatre-goers in evening dress): talk to both for different hints. Use a generic name, not "Au Pied de Cochon" (trademark, still trading).

**8. Telephone token (jeton) in a café** · token from 1937 · cafés · B (object); **Not found** for use in 1967
- PTT token, copper-nickel, 18 mm, deep square groove with "P.T.T"; later demonetised (date not stated).
- Source: [Numista 16650](https://en.numista.com/16650)
- Game: buy a jeton at the counter to use the café phone (already in). Keep the dialogue non-committal until a source confirms 1967 use.

**9. Waiting years for a home telephone** · 1960s-70s · France · B
- "From 1935 to 1968 almost no new equipment was installed"; waits up to seven years (1976); in 1962 the PTT minister called the phone a "gimmick". A PTT leaflet (April 1963) explains to the public why they must wait.
- Sources: [TIME, 7 Jun 1976](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,947731,00.html); [PTT leaflet April 1963](https://www.histelfrance.fr/storage/2025/06/1963.04NoticeAttenteTelephone.pdf)
- Game: **why everyone uses the café phone**: NPCs have no phone at home; messages go through the café, the concierge or a pneumatique. Explains the existing quest design.

**10. Rear-platform buses** · last run 22 Jan 1971 · bus lines · A
- Open rear platform; last platform buses withdrawn 22 Jan 1971 (line 21, Saint-Lazare - Porte de Gentilly).
- Sources: [Paris.fr, bus history](https://www.paris.fr/pages/les-bus-parisiens-cinq-siecles-d-histoire-16339); [Wikipedia, Renault TN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_TN) (TN6 out 1969, TN4F 1970, TN4H 1971)
- Game: **hop on/off the moving platform** to cross the map (timing mini-game), or a cutscene transition. What riders did on the platform (stand, smoke) is **not sourced**.

**11. Métro first class (yellow-marked cars)** · 1900-1991 · métro · A
- 1st-class cars had yellow markings (one source: at the ends of the train; Slate: in the middle — **conflict**); leather seats vs wooden slats; tickets: 2nd class **havane (tan)**, 1st class **green** from 1958; 1958 carnet 30 F / 45 F (old francs). Ended 30 Jul / 1 Aug 1991 (sources differ by 2 days).
- Sources: [Paris ZigZag](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/metro-rer-premiere-classe-paris/); [Slate](https://www.slate.fr/story/143300/metro-premiere-classe); [Paris ZigZag, ticket](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/petite-histoire-du-ticket-de-metro-parisien/); Paris.fr
- Game: **wrong-class fine**: board the yellow car with a tan ticket, a controller appears. The 1967 ticket colours are assumed from 1958 (C).

**12. Concierge and the night door** · 1960s · every building · A (1964)
- ~64,000 Paris concierges in 1964, ~95% women; on duty 6 a.m.-10 p.m.; garbage, locking doors; the old **"cordon"** (rope pulled from bed to open the street door) was abolished by a 1957 law, most doors now opened by an **electric release**.
- Source: [TIME, 17 Jan 1964](https://time.com/archive/6813310/france-but-who-will-be-concierge-to-the-concierges/)
- Game: **concierge as gatekeeper NPC**: knows everyone's mail and comings and goings; at night the player must wake her to be buzzed in. Don't show a rope in 1967 (it was abolished).

**13. Bicycle police in capes ("hirondelles")** · 1901-1984 · streets · B
- Named after their flying capes; 2,819 cycle police in 1950, three brigades per arrondissement, often at night; disappeared through the 1980s, ended 1984.
- Source: [Paris ZigZag](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/hirondelles-police-paris/)
- Game: **patrol to avoid or to ask for directions**; a cape silhouette is a great 8×16 sprite. 1967 numbers not sourced.

**14. Colour TV arrives (1 Oct 1967)** · 1967 · cafés, shop windows · A
- Colour started 1 Oct 1967 at 14:15 (SECAM) on the 2nd channel; on-air announcers (speakerines) presented programmes until the early 1990s; no TV advertising until 1968; third channel only in Dec 1972.
- Sources: [Ozap 2017](https://www.ozap.com/actu/50-ans-de-la-television-en-couleurs-france-2-va-ressusciter-les-speakerines/523574); [Wikipedia, ORTF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_de_Radiodiffusion_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9vision_Fran%C3%A7aise)
- Game: if the episode is set after 1 Oct 1967, a crowd outside a shop window watching **the first colour set**; otherwise NPCs talk about it coming. Fixes the episode month question.

**15. Vespasiennes (street urinals)** · disappearing from the 1960s, massively in the 1980s · streets · B
- Replaced by JCDecaux sanisettes; the last one survives at 86 bd Arago.
- Source: [Paris ZigZag](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/vespasienne-derniere/)
- Game: scenery / a hiding spot. Tone: keep it light.

**16. Paid chairs in the public gardens** · until 1974 (Luxembourg) · gardens · A (official)
- Chairs were rented through concessions since the 18th century; the Senate made Luxembourg chairs free in 1974.
- Source: [Senate, Jardin du Luxembourg](https://jardin.senat.fr/en/history-heritage/heritage/the-seats.html)
- Game: **sit down, pay the chair-woman**. Note: a female collector ("chaisière") is **not** in this source; Sortiraparis's article did not mention fees.

**17. Scopitone (film jukebox)** · 1958-late 1960s · bars · B
- Jukebox with a 16 mm film; made by Cameca (Courbevoie); faded by the end of the 1960s. Presence in Paris cafés **not stated** in the source read.
- Source: [Wikipedia, Scopitone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopitone)
- Game: a coin-operated machine playing an **original** tune with a tiny animated film. Generic name ("film jukebox") unless legal OK.

### Tier 2: good scenery, side quests, dialogue

- Sources: [Sortiraparis](https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-visit-in-paris/history-heritage/articles/331385-did-you-know-the-grande-halle-de-la-villette-once-housed-a-huge-slaughterhouse); [Paris.fr, lieux disparus](https://www.paris.fr/pages/ces-lieux-disparus-qui-font-aussi-l-histoire-de-paris-2-2-32975)

**19. Horse market of Vaugirard** · moved 1907, closed 1976 · 15th arr. · B

- Source: [Paris ZigZag](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/lieux-insolites/les-vestiges-des-anciens-marches-aux-chevaux-parisiens/)

**20. Bercy wine warehouses** · decline from 1960s; leases frozen 1964; last cellars destroyed 1993 · 12th arr. · A

- Source: [Wikipedia, Wine warehouses of Bercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_warehouses_of_Bercy)

**21. Seine barges (cargo, families aboard)** · 1960s · the river · **Not found** for 1967 detail
